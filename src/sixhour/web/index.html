Section 1 · The law

Two clocks start the moment you notice.

Clock AIn force since 2022

6hours

CERT-In incident report

Every organisation in India must report listed cyber incidents to CERT-In within six hours of noticing them. Ransomware, data breaches, phishing, unauthorised access and 16 other categories are on the list.

CERT-In Directions, 28 April 2022 · IT Act s.70B(6)

Clock BIn force 13 May 2027

72hours

Personal data breach report

If personal data was touched, the Data Protection Board and every affected person must be told without delay, with a detailed report to the Board inside 72 hours. The penalty for failing to give notice runs up to Rs 200 crore.

DPDP Act 2023 · DPDP Rules 2025, Rule 7

A large company has a security operations centre and a law firm on call for this. A 40-person exporter, diagnostic lab or loan agency has one IT person and a folder of logs nobody can read.

Section 2 · The desk

What happens after you paste the logs.

  1. 1

    Personal data is blacked out first

    Aadhaar, PAN, phone, email and card numbers are masked before a single line reaches an AI model. The same value always gets the same token, so the trail can still be followed. IP addresses stay: they are the evidence.

  2. 2

    The incident is reconstructed, with proof

    A rules engine and Gemini work out what happened, when, from where, on which systems, and whether personal data was touched. Every event on the timeline points at the log line that shows it. No line, no claim.

  3. 3

    Both clocks start, and stay in front of you

    The six-hour CERT-In clock always runs. The 72-hour DPDP clock starts only if the logs show personal data was involved. Close the tab, come back, and they are still counting.

  4. 4

    The filings are drafted

    The CERT-In incident report, the Data Protection Board intimation and the notice to affected people, in plain language and in the customer's language: Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Bengali or Gujarati. Anything the logs cannot establish is marked for a person to confirm. It is never guessed.

  5. 5

    Your IT person gets the next hour's checklist

    Concrete containment steps, most urgent first: which machine to pull off the network, which account to lock, which address to block.

Section 3 · Standing orders

Built to be checked, not trusted.

  1. A person files. Always. SixHour drafts. It never sends anything to a regulator or a customer on its own.
  2. No citation, no claim. Evidence that points at a line that is not in your logs is thrown away before you see it.
  3. The model cannot erase a finding. If the rules engine proved personal data was touched, an AI answer cannot quietly remove it.
  4. The clock never waits for AI. If Gemini is slow or down, the rules engine result is shown instead, and says so.
  5. Nothing is stored. No account, no database. The open incident lives in your browser until you clear it.

Section 6 · Questions

Asked before the first pilot.

Is this legal advice?

No. The drafts follow the structure of the CERT-In incident reporting form and DPDP Rule 7, and they are drafting aids. A person at your business, ideally with your lawyer or CA, reviews and files them.

Does SixHour send the report to CERT-In for me?

No, by design. You copy, download or print the draft and file it yourself. The CERT-In draft carries the address it goes to.

What happens to the logs I paste?

They are masked on our server, analysed, and discarded when the response is sent. Nothing is written to a database. When AI analysis is on, the masked lines go to Google's Gemini API. You can switch AI off on the desk and use the rules engine alone. Full detail here.

Which logs does it understand?

Windows security events, web server access logs, Google Workspace audit logs and firewall or syslog lines. The rules engine looks for brute-force logins, ransomware behaviour, SQL injection, phishing sign-ins, hidden mail rules, bulk record access and unusual outbound transfers. Gemini reads everything else in context.

The DPDP rules are not in force yet. Why show that clock?

The breach-reporting duty under Rule 7 starts on 13 May 2027. The CERT-In six-hour duty has applied since 2022. Showing both today means the process is practised before the second one carries a penalty.

What if the AI gets it wrong?

Every timeline event links to its log line, so a wrong claim is visible in one click. Claims without a real line are removed, and the deterministic rules engine runs on every incident as a second opinion.
